# FW-60 — Critical-stability route

## 1. Status and purpose

- **Status:** normative research contract; not a theorem.
- **Active question:** Q-0018.
- **Primary strategy:** Route B.
- **Route A:** suspended as a main route.

This framework specifies what a legitimate critical-stability proof must preserve
and what it is forbidden to assume. It does not define a defect functional by
fiat and does not assert that the required compactness or structure theorem
already exists.

## 2. Strategic distinction

Route A seeks a near-lossless transport of nearly all failure mass into separate
root, slot, real-edge, token, quotient, or closure capacities.

Route B seeks a stability dichotomy:

\[
\operatorname{Def}(\mathcal X)\text{ has positive density}
\quad\vee\quad
\mathcal X\text{ is close to an exact critical object}.
\]

The critical part is not required to receive an independent charging
entitlement. It must instead be classified and shown to produce an IT, a real
near-\(1/4\) link, or a complete-block contradiction.

## 3. Faithful global execution object

A candidate execution object \(\mathcal X\) must retain, at every state or atom:

1. the actual blocks and actual selected vertices;
2. actual blocker-edge identity and provenance;
3. root projection and any root/configuration slot;
4. actual pivot and released endpoint;
5. carrier support;
6. complete genealogy;
7. all sampled future coordinates needed to define future completeness;
8. the separate root, slot, and global-real-edge ledger histories.

Refinement may split mass but may not copy it. Two histories may be identified
only by an accepted future-compatible quotient preserving actual support and all
legal successors.

## 4. Natural defect requirements

A candidate nonnegative defect may include independently meaningful terms such
as:

- an exact entropy/information-loss term for irreversible state merging;
- the F-0038 near-critical deficit;
- mass of non-normal local exchange windows;
- actual-support inconsistency under a specified splice test;
- mass lost by an independently proved faithful projection or compactness map;
- a real boundary or multi-defect term with a stated combinatorial meaning.

A candidate defect may not be defined as:

- distance from “the desired binary forest” without an independent metric;
- failure to have product support;
- failure to have an accepted terminal exit;
- uncharged heavy excess;
- the mass of whatever remains after the current proof attempt.

Every term must have:

1. a natural combinatorial or probabilistic definition;
2. covariance under faithful refinement and genealogy expansion;
3. a zero-set that can be tested on finite exact models;
4. a separate proof of any claimed dissipation consequence.

## 5. Zero-defect target

The first structural target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{binary regeneration forest}
+
\text{reversible exact-future cores}.
}
\]

The “forest” is an actual genealogy expansion, not merely a universal cover of a
compressed phase graph. A reversible core may remain; zero information loss
alone only implies invertibility/permutation behavior.

The exact theorem must state how actual support, blocker edges, pivots, roots,
and future choices are represented in both pieces.

## 6. Reversible-core target

For every positive-mass reversible core, prove at least one:

1. actual-support splice saturation leading to a Cartesian product;
2. a genuine single-pivot critical-link component;
3. an augmentation or survivor;
4. a complete real-block no-IT subinstance;
5. a strictly positive natural defect.

Reversibility, phase consistency, projected surjectivity, or local same-pivot
data alone are not accepted substitutes.

## 7. Required finite test families

Every proposed defect and zero-defect axiom must be tested against:

1. all normal \(Q_4\) matching models;
2. the F-0029 differing-pivot model;
3. the three-orientation reset model A-0028;
4. fixed-light-anchor reversible cores;
5. diagonal codebooks and dispersed-incidence examples;
6. examples with actual product support and a near-\(1/4\) pivot link.

Passing these tests does not prove the general theorem; failing one refutes the
candidate definition or lemma.

## 8. Route-A interface

The following may be used as supporting results:

- configuration auditor and separate ledgers;
- future-complete lift;
- aggregate pair-cylinder normalization;
- local Hall-deficiency orthogonalization;
- same-load exchange flow;
- old-anchor Lyapunov;
- orientation progress;
- F-0034 and F-0042 as conditional endgames.

They do not impose a near-lossless charging acceptance criterion on Route B.

## 9. Progress and stopping rules

A Route-B result counts as main progress only if it supplies at least one:

- a faithful compactness/tightness theorem;
- a natural defect with a proved zero-set property;
- an exact zero-defect normal form;
- actual-support reversible-core saturation;
- a real critical-link product theorem;
- a quantitative stability modulus;
- a real counterexample to one of these statements.

Stop and record a failure instead of extending the taxonomy when the next step
only introduces a new cut, token, quotient candidate, or obstruction name.

## 10. Nonclaims

This framework does not prove:

- Q-0018, Q-0017, or Q-0016;
- that the global limit is a pure tree;
- that reversible cores have product support;
- that local same-pivot mass glues globally;
- that splice is free;
- that all residual mass is small or chargeable;
- the one-quarter theorem.
