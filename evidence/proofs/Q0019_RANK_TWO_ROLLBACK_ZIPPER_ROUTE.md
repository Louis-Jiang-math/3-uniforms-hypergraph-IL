# Q-0019 rank-two rollback and normal-zipper route

## 0. Status and purpose

This note records the strongest currently auditable reduction of `S1 / Q-0019`
obtained from the supplied proof-gap conversations and the new bounded
rank-two computation.  It does **not** close Q-0019 and does not replace the
fork--inverse-fiber--anchor umbrella route.

The main conceptual change is that the remaining backend should no longer be
phrased as arbitrary descendant-edge migration.  The useful reductions are
actual-state reductions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{common-parent parallel repair}
\to
\text{minimal bad pair/triple}
\to
\text{exact rollback}
\to
\text{low pair grammar or rank-two high core}
\to
\text{normal zipper / actual-edge collision frontier}.
}
\]

The finite normal-Q4 statements are generated by
`enumerate/q0019_rank_two_zipper_validation.py`.  Claims labelled *conditional*
below are record-language or execution-interface statements: they are usable
only after their stated faithful source interface is supplied.

## 1. The inherited source/output gap remains valid

For weighted disjoint-blocker fork mass at pivot `x`, write

\[
2F_x\le d_H(x)\ell_x^*,
\qquad
\ell_x^*=\max_{e\ni x}\sum_f w_x(e,f).
\]

Local refill estimates on a later output edge do not imply the needed source
load estimate.  A source row of mass `W` may be partitioned into `b^2`
descendant certificates of mass `W/b^2` while retaining source load `W`.
Therefore all new constructions below must preserve owner, root, source
occurrence, and actual-edge provenance.  No finite normal form is by itself a
source charge.

## 2. Common-parent parallel repair

Fix an independent parent transversal `R` and four hole blocks
`A_1,...,A_4`.  For each attempted value `q_i in A_i`, define its canonical
singleton repair relative to the **same** parent:

\[
R_i(q_i)=(R\setminus D_i(q_i))\cup\{q_i\},
\]

where `D_i(q_i) subseteq R` and every singleton state is independent.  For
`I subseteq [4]` define

\[
R_I(Q)=
\left(R\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}D_i(q_i)\right)
\cup\{q_i:i\in I\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The essential point is that all deletion sets are functions of the common
source parent and the attempted value; there is no sequential private-witness
reset.

### Lemma 2.1 — minimal bad sets have size two or three

Assume every singleton state `R_{\{i\}}` is independent.  If `I` is
inclusion-minimal with `R_I` nonindependent, then

\[
|I|\in\{2,3\}.
\]

**Proof.** Let `g subseteq R_I` be a three-edge witnessing nonindependence.
For every `i in I`, minimality implies `g` is not contained in
`R_{I\setminus\{i\}}`; hence `q_i in g`.  Thus `|I|<=3`.  Since singleton
states are independent, `|I|>=2`. ∎

This is a genuine dimensional reduction: a four-target interaction never
needs an arbitrary high-arity local obstruction.

### Lemma 2.2 — exact branch rollback

Let `I` be inclusion-minimal bad and `t in I`.  Remove `q_t` and restore the
entire branch deletion set `D_t(q_t)`; rollback any spectator branch in the
same way.  The resulting actual state is exactly

\[
R_{I\setminus\{t\}}(Q),
\]

and is independent by minimality.

**Proof.** Expanding (2.1), removing the `t`-branch contribution deletes
`q_t` and removes `D_t(q_t)` from the union of deleted parent vertices.  This
is exactly the definition of `R_{I\setminus\{t\}}`. ∎

This eliminates the earlier support-synchronization problem: one need not
prove `D_t(q)=D_t(q')` along a cylinder.  The entire branch is undone.

### Nonclaim 2.3 — rollback is not irreversible entropy loss

The same hole block may later be sampled again.  Therefore

\[
4\to2\quad\text{or}\quad4\to1
\]

at one rollback event does **not** imply a `b^{-2}` or `b^{-3}` record saving.
A valid credit must either permanently remove a future obligation, enter a
source-owned telescoping potential, or be paid by an actual bounded resource.

## 3. Pair-state grammar and the true high-node requirement

The conversations isolate a two-sided pair genealogy.  In the low grammar,
each side has matching rank at most one.  If a rank-zero side contributes
`1` and a rank-one side contributes one actual edge label and one pair child,
`Delta z`, the two-sided low polynomial is

\[
L(z)=(1+\Delta z)^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\inf_{z>0}\frac{L(z)}{z}=4\Delta.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This exactly reproduces the one-quarter threshold at the low-rank level.
Consequently the only place requiring an additional asymptotically vanishing
mark is a **high node**, where at least one side has matching rank at least two.

A convenient analytic envelope used in the conversation is

\[
\Phi_y(z)=
(1+\Delta z)^2
+y\left[(1-\Delta z)^{-2}-(1+\Delta z)^2\right].
\tag{3.3}
\]

A fixed positive `y` changes the leading growth constant; recovering
`4 Delta + o(Delta)` requires

\[
y=o_b(1).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus a constant-scale protected mark is insufficient even if it is perfectly
faithful.

### Conditional reduction 3.1 — rank two is the irreducible high core

If a high side has rank `r>=2`, take its first two canonical matching edges as
the rank-two core and leave the remaining `r-2` pair obligations untouched.
Any owner/root-preserving no-copy macro that handles the first two edges without
creating a new high obligation lifts to all `r>=2`.  Therefore the irreducible
high geometry is the `(2,0)` / `(0,2)` core.

This is a reduction of combinatorial arity, not yet a proof that a child is
cancelled.

## 4. Protected-token first use: what it proves and what it cannot prove

A useful correction from the conversations is that a protected token need not
survive forever.  It may first be deleted by the **main canonical matching
repair**, because that repair already records the actual matching edge used to
delete it.

Suppose `N` source-owned tokens are born.  Let `C` already-recorded matching
edges later delete `2C` of them in pairs, and let `S=N-2C` tokens survive to the
macro boundary.  Under the record model in which each token birth contributes
one `b`-ary coordinate and each deleting matching edge contributes one `Delta`
choice, the token inverse multiplicity is

\[
\frac{\Delta^C}{b^N}
=
\left(\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{b}\right)^{2C}b^{-S}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `Delta>=1`, putting

\[
\vartheta_b=\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{b}
\]

gives

\[
\frac{\Delta^C}{b^N}\le\vartheta_b^N.
\tag{4.2}
\]

At `Delta <= (1/4-epsilon)b^2`,

\[
\vartheta_b\le\sqrt{1/4-\varepsilon}<1/2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

So first-use deletion by an already-paid matching edge is decoder-safe in this
model.  Tokens may come from different birth events; only no-copy deletion is
required.

However, if every high node creates only a fixed `t=O(1)` number of such tokens,
then near the threshold

\[
\vartheta_b^t\to2^{-t},
\]

which is a nonzero constant.  By (3.4), this cannot by itself produce the
needed `o_b(1)` high-node mark.

Hence the protected-token construction is a **bookkeeping module**, not the
closing quantitative mechanism.  The closing mechanism must remove a future
child obligation or force actual resource reuse.

## 5. Protected-pair stopper and why degree counting is insufficient

Exact rollback naturally suggests protecting retained targets.  A new attempt
`q` can be repaired by deleting inherited/unprotected vertices unless a blocker
has two already protected endpoints:

\[
\{q,p_1,p_2\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is the first genuine stopper of the monotone protected process.

But complete coverage of a hole block by protected pairs does not imply a
quadratic degree.  Take `A={q_1,...,q_b}` and `2b` protected vertices in distinct
blocks and include only

\[
\{q_i,p_{2i-1},p_{2i}\},\qquad i=1,...,b.
\]

Then every `q_i` is blocked by a protected pair while `Delta=1`.
Therefore a protected-pair exit needs a genealogy/collision argument, not bare
degree counting.

This observation motivates returning the fresh protected-pair tree to the low
pair grammar (3.1): replacing the two protected endpoints creates two holes.
Only a later high-rank event needs new treatment.

## 6. Finite normal-Q4 switching geometry

The bounded generator exhausts all coordinate perfect matchings of `Q4`.  The
existing counts remain

\[
272\text{ matchings},\qquad8\text{ normal matchings},
\]

with 192 normal rooted independent one-hole states and 768 two-branch release
pairs.

### Observation 6.1 — shared-q switching rule

At every normal root, the blockers of the two binary completions intersect in
one actual coordinate-value `q`.  The four two-branch release choices satisfy

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &q\text{ retained on right}&q\text{ released on right}\\ \hline
q\text{ retained on left}&C&S\\
q\text{ released on left}&S&R
\end{array}
\tag{6.1}
\]

with 192 occurrences of each cell.  Thus the two mixed choices are exactly the
384 splice cases.  There is no fifth local continuation type in this bounded
normal model.

### Observation 6.2 — unique eight-edge zipper

Every splice case has exactly one eight-edge bridge order and seven
edge-disjoint meeting cuts of that same order:

\[
1+7,\ 2+6,\ 3+5,\ 4+4,\ 5+3,\ 6+2,\ 7+1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The eight actual blocker triples form one cyclic `8_3` incidence configuration.
After cyclic relabelling `v_0,...,v_7`, one orientation has

\[
H_i=\{v_{i-1},v_i,v_{i+2}\},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z_8.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Each vertex has degree three; `H_i` is disjoint only from `H_{i+4}`.

### Lemma 6.3 — same-edge reversal

A one-hole transition using actual blocker edge `g` has a unique reverse
transition using the same `g`: reinsert the released coordinate value, recover
the same full word and blocker, then release the old hole coordinate.  Thus the
normal zipper is a finite reversible connector at the state-transition level.

This does **not** imply a merger of two future obligations.

## 7. Endpoint incidence model and support completion

Write the unique bridge as `H_0,...,H_7` and set

\[
e_1=H_0,\quad e_2=H_7,\quad f_1=H_1,\quad f_2=H_6,
\qquad\{q\}=e_1\cap e_2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

All 384 splice cases have one unoriented endpoint/first-inward incidence type.
A representative form is

\[
\begin{aligned}
e_1&=\{q,r,s\},\\
e_2&=\{q,a,b\},\\
f_1&=\{r,a,z_1\},\\
f_2&=\{r,b,z_2\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

with the displayed seven vertices distinct.  Hence `e_1,e_2` expose five of the
eight binary coordinate-values; adding `f_1,f_2` exposes seven.

In the complete `b`-ary completion universe, after fixing the endpoint pair the
three complementary values give `(b-1)^3` completions; adding the first-inward
pair leaves `b-1`; a suitable central completion determines the last one.
Thus the tuple `(e_1,e_2;q;f_1,f_2)` is a **codimension-one support certificate**.

The missing `b`-ary value is not yet an entropy saving.  It becomes a valid
`1/b` mark only if its recovery is source-owned or occurs through an
already-paid faithful event.

## 8. Strict endpoint persistence is false; support persistence is the right finite invariant

Let `s` be a normal rooted state and `\bar s` the antipodal state obtained by
keeping the hole block and flipping the other three binary values.  The
canonical mixed zipper sends `s` to `\bar s`.

The exhaustive computation gives, in all 192 rooted cases:

- the same eight-edge support is used at `s` and `\bar s`;
- the endpoint edge pair is disjoint from its old pair;
- the common coordinate-value changes;
- relative to the old bridge, the endpoints move from positions `(0,7)` to
  `(3,4)`;
- applying the antipodal map twice returns to the original rooted state.

Therefore the proposed invariant `(e_1,e_2;q)` is **not** source-static.
Within a fixed normal binary window, the eight-edge support `M` is the stronger
finite invariant.

Moreover the 24 rooted independent states are in bijection with the 24
intersecting unordered edge pairs of the `8_3` support.  An ordered endpoint
pair reconstructs the rooted state and belongs to a unique normal support in
the binary window.

## 9. Support packing: four in a binary window, twelve in a ternary box

Across the eight normal supports of a fixed binary window:

- all 32 possible blocker triples occur;
- each triple belongs to exactly two normal supports;
- two distinct supports intersect in 0 or 2 blocker triples;
- the overlap-2 graph is exactly `K_{4,4}`.

Hence a pairwise actual-edge-disjoint normal-support family in one binary window
has size at most four.

For `[3]^4`, all 81 binary windows and eight normal types produce 648 distinct
embedded normal supports on 108 possible blocker triples.  The generator
verifies that the full block/value automorphism group is transitive on these
648 supports.  Exact finite MILP gives

\[
\boxed{\nu_{\rm normal}([3]^4)=12.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

A 12-packing exists; a 13-packing is infeasible after fixing one support, which
is valid by transitivity.

This is a real finite resource bound: a source-static ternary four-buffer can
contain only twelve completely fresh normal zipper supports before some actual
blocker edge is reused.  What remains open is the bridge from this local
support reuse to a uniform owner-weighted Q-0019 capacity bound.

## 10. Why a zipper is not yet `P^2 -> P`

The normal zipper proves a confluence statement at the **state** level.  Two
histories can reach a common midpoint.  But two pair children may still have
different owner/root/stack/provenance obligations after that state.

Therefore

\[
\text{same actual state}\not\Rightarrow\text{same future obligation}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

A faithful cancellation macro must show one of:

1. **exact-future merge:** the two open boundaries have identical future
   interfaces and one can be deleted without copying or merging histories;
2. **bounded return:** a source-static finite support can be used only `O(1)`
   times before an already-recorded actual edge is reused;
3. **independent paid exit:** a real capacity/information term pays the second
   obligation.

The current finite evidence strongly supports option 2 inside a ternary
source-static box, but the owner-weighted global recurrence is not yet proved.

## 11. Current Q-0019 frontier

The strongest current target is no longer “classify one-coordinate migration.”
It is the following source-interface statement.

> **Rank-two support-return problem.** Starting from an actual high `(2,0)` or
> `(0,2)` pair core with full source provenance, construct a no-copy record
> backend such that every normal continuation either (i) closes an open future
> obligation exactly, or (ii) consumes one of finitely many source-static
> normal supports and any later support reuse is paid by an actual-edge
> collision.  Nonnormal/minimal-bad branches must enter exact rollback or an
> independently valid defect/exit.  No retained pair child may be charged again
> merely for being present.

A satisfactory recurrence must combine the low diagonal growth `4 Delta` with
a strictly subexponential number of free high events per source owner, or an
`o_b(1)` owner-weighted high mark.  The finite bound `12` is evidence for the
former but is not yet a theorem about the actual genealogy.

## 12. Do-not-repeat constraints extracted from the conversations

The following shortcuts have been explicitly ruled out and should not re-enter
Q-0019 under new names:

- descendant output codimension implies source fork-load codimension;
- a retained/frozen pair child supplies a new `1/b` or `1/b^2` mark;
- a minimal triple's third target is automatically irreversible;
- fixed support implies a fixed pair fiber;
- fresh transition rank or fresh Shannon information automatically telescopes
  across changing supports;
- normal-Q4 density by itself is a source survival probability;
- rollback of a branch permanently removes that hole obligation;
- complete protected-pair coverage implies quadratic maximum degree;
- the endpoint tuple `(e_1,e_2;q)` is fixed across a zipper;
- two histories meeting at one state are automatically one future obligation.

These are design constraints, not merely historical commentary.
